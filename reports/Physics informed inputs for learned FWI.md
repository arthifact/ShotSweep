# Feed the network evidence, not flat-layer answers

Physics-derived inputs for velocity networks have been tried many times, but no published study compares raw gathers with physics channels at matched network, data and compute across the OpenFWI families, so your FlatVel-A result (25 models with physics channels at MAE 0.096 beating 452 raw-gather models at 0.113) is already one of very few controlled data points. What has measurably worked elsewhere is narrower than the green-screen analogy suggests: extended images beat plain migrated images when the background velocity is poor (**SSIM 0.63 vs 0.52** on Compass, but only 0.87 vs 0.85 on OpenFWI CurveFault-A), sharper background-velocity views lift B-family SSIM by up to 0.08 (but only with oracle backgrounds taken from the true model), L1 plus MS-SSIM beats L1 or L2 alone, adversarial training raises average cross-family SSIM from 0.404 to 0.583, and putting the forward operator inside the network beats feeding the network its output (+2.2 dB in CT), while the largest OpenFWI gains on record come from data diversity (+28.6% held-out-family MAE from training on all families). Your current channels go silent on CurveFault-B and Style-B because they hard-code FlatVel-A's geology as answers (hyperbolic moveout, no velocity inversions, a fixed 1.13 jump into the bottom layer), whereas about half the interfaces in the Vel-B families are inversions and Style-B has no coherent reflectors at all. The pipeline proposed here replaces those geology-specific estimates with physics operators valid in any medium, the central one being per-shot wave-equation migration over a scan of background velocities, which is the seismic version of the multi-view-stereo plane sweep; it tags every channel with where it is valid, adds a free data test of the flat-layer assumption, fuses shots, backgrounds and frequency bands with gated attention and modality dropout, recomputes the physics once at the network's own first estimate, and refines with full physics at test time only after a half-period cycle-skip check. Expect physics channels to buy data efficiency and robustness on unseen geology rather than in-distribution accuracy at full OpenFWI scale; the staged plan, with sample- and compute-matched learning curves and leave-one-geology-out tests, is built to confirm or falsify exactly that.

## Physics inputs exist in pieces; the matched-budget test does not

The dominant published pattern feeds a migrated image together with the background velocity used to compute it, predicts a velocity update, then remigrates and repeats, so the network acts as a learned tomographic operator rather than a global data-to-model map. Deep-tomography fed 14 subsurface-offset panels, computed by RTM in a Gaussian-smoothed model, to three successive U-Nets trained on about 720 synthetic models and reached **Marmousi MAE 185–235 m/s and SSIM 0.59–0.68** on a model it never saw ([Muller et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.10813)). Zhang and Gao used RTM images and common-source migration gathers plus the migration velocity and claim better accuracy and generalization than raw prestack input, without numbers in the abstracts ([TGRS 2021](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/TGRS.2021.3062688); [GRSL 2022](https://scholar.xjtu.edu.cn/en/publications/high-resolution-velocity-model-building-based-on-common-source-mi/)). TGS and PGS feed depth-migrated gathers plus velocity to an FNO-CNN trained on about 50,000 synthetic models and resolve the macro model in two to five remigration passes ([Korsmo et al.](https://www.tgs.com/hubfs/2025%20IMAGE%20Preliminary/FNO_MVA_Korsmo_final_pdf.pdf)), yet TGS also reports that synthetic pretraining alone did not generalize to field data until a self-supervised residual-moveout loss was added ([Roberts et al.](https://www.tgs.com/hubfs/Technical%20Library/EAGE%202026/2026Roberts_ML_VMB_EAGE_v1.pdf)). KAUST's conditional diffusion model, conditioned on a smooth background, an RTM image and well logs with conditions dropped at random during training, reports in-distribution **MAE 33.9 m/s and SSIM 0.942**, and Marmousi MAE 147 m/s before FWI and 110 m/s after ([Cheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26482)).

The cleanest controlled comparisons pit one physics input against another rather than against raw data. WISE conditioned the same normalizing flow either on subsurface-offset common-image gathers or on RTM images, both computed in a 1D background, and the extended gathers won: **SSIM 0.87 vs 0.85 on OpenFWI CurveFault-A** with 2,800 training models, and **0.63 vs 0.52 on Compass** ([Yin et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06230)). The CurveFault-A gap is small because the background there was the horizontally averaged true model, which left plain RTM nearly kinematically correct; extensions pay off when the background is wrong. Ray-parameter gathers migrated at a constant 2 km/s, a physics transform that needs no velocity estimate at all, reached SSIM 0.946 from only 140 training sections ([Geng et al.](https://academic.oup.com/gji/article/228/2/1054/6372247)), and semblance cubes gave SSIM about 0.89 on layered models and 0.81 with salt ([Araya-Polo et al.](https://sep.stanford.edu/sep/joseph29/pubs/Araya-2018-dltomo.pdf)). Fabien-Ouellet and Sarkar rejected semblance as lossy and built its scan-and-stack logic into the architecture instead, yet their interval-velocity RMSE rose from 323 m/s to as much as **424 m/s once dips reached only 6°** ([Fabien-Ouellet & Sarkar](https://publications.polymtl.ca/6302/1/2020_Fabien-Ouellet_Seismic_velocity_estimation_deep_recurrent.pdf)), the same flat-layer fragility your channels show. Only one network has been fed a scan of images at many trial velocities: a CNN scored 161 residual migrations for focusing and, under limited aperture, beat semblance with **fault-segmentation IoU 0.83 vs 0.72** after fine-tuning on just 56 labelled field patches ([Jennings et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.03646)).

Background velocity is the most powerful extra "view" measured on OpenFWI, but only with oracle inputs: in DiffusionVel the background is the true model low-pass filtered by a Gaussian, and shrinking that kernel from 49 to 9 cells raised CurveFault-B SSIM from 0.706 to 0.788 and FlatFault-B from 0.846 to 0.882 ([Zhang, Li & Huang](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21776v1)). Conditioning on a geology class and one well log lifts diffusion-regularized FWI from SSIM 0.900 to 0.976 on OpenFWI-derived tests ([CD-FWI](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06959v2)). Multi-shot fusion, by contrast, is almost unexplored: every OpenFWI baseline stacks the five shots as input channels, and no OpenFWI network was found that encodes shots separately and fuses them with attention. The nearest evidence is InversionNet3D's grouped convolution over sources, whose 3D accuracy barely moves across three different 8-of-25 source subsets (MAE 0.0105–0.0108) ([OpenFWI](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.02926)). In the 2025 Kaggle competition, exact re-simulated mirror images beat naive flip augmentation because only the central shot breaks the mirror symmetry of the source layout ([4th place write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/waveform-inversion/writeups/greysnow-4th-place-solution-for-the-gwi-competitio)).

Losses tell a consistent story: physics in the loss mostly buys the right to use unlabeled data, while robust pixel losses and distribution-level losses buy structure. UPFWI, which puts a differentiable finite-difference simulator in the loss, matched supervised InversionNet on FlatFault only by training on 48K unlabeled samples against InversionNet's 24K labelled ones, and its VGG perceptual term on the seismic data cut velocity MSE by **43%** (2999.6 to 1705.4) relative to a pixel-only data loss ([Jin et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.07584v2)). In the final OpenFWI benchmark UPFWI is worse than InversionNet on every 2D family while costing 30–60 h on 32 GPUs against 2–5.5 h on one ([OpenFWI](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.02926)). VelocityGAN's adversarial term gives the largest cheap generalization gain on record: averaged over the off-diagonal entries of OpenFWI's cross-family tables, SSIM is **0.583 for VelocityGAN, 0.518 for UPFWI and 0.404 for InversionNet**, though it is unstable on FlatFault-A (MAE 0.0868 vs 0.0172) ([OpenFWI](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.02926)). On velocity itself, L1 plus MS-SSIM gave SSIM 0.946 against 0.920 for L1 and 0.888 for L2 ([Geng et al.](https://academic.oup.com/gji/article/228/2/1054/6372247)), matching the image-restoration finding that networks trained with L1 reach lower L2 error than networks trained with L2 ([Zhao et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1511.08861)). No OpenFWI ablation of gradient, SSIM or perceptual losses on velocity at matched data exists.

Test-time physics helps when the starting model already sits in the right basin and fails when it does not. Latent-space gradient descent on a pretrained generator cut aggregate OpenFWI MAE from 0.0159 to 0.0136 ([Feng et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00377v2)), and FWI reparameterized through a network cut Marmousi2 MAPE from 6.71% to 4.17% ([DR-FWI](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17375v2)). A foundation-model feature misfit followed by L2 took 3D Overthrust relative error from 0.266 to 0.056 where L2 alone stalled at 0.240, and switching to L2 too early reintroduced cycle skipping ([Alfarhan et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05763)). Diffusion-guided refinement on the OpenFWI B families more than halves FWI's error (CurveFault-B MAE **112 vs 264 m/s**, SSIM 0.860 vs 0.603), but it starts from the Gaussian-smoothed true model ([PSG](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12899)). No paper reports the obvious non-oracle pipeline, a network prediction used as the FWI start, with OpenFWI metrics.

Two cross-cutting results set expectations. Data diversity dominates the OpenFWI record: training on all ten families improves MAE by 13% in distribution and 28.6% on held-out families ([BigFWI](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.15388)), and a 1B-parameter tokenized model trained on about 5M generated pairs reaches aggregate MAE 0.0136 against 0.0432 for BigFWI-L ([Feng et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00377v2)). In other fields, physically meaningful channels mainly close the gap between training and test performance. Mid-level vision features cut data needs by an order of magnitude, but the advantage vanished once training randomization covered the test domain ([Sax et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.11971)); ground-truth depth and segmentation channels roughly doubled driving success (0.35 to 0.67) while predicted versions of the same channels gave 0.56 ([Zhou et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1905.12887)); naive multi-input fusion did worse than the best single input (71.4% vs 72.6%) until per-input loss balancing lifted it to 74.7% ([Wang et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1905.12681)); and in CT, putting the operator inside the network beat feeding it a physics image by 2.2 dB, while initializing with the physics image only sped up training ([Adler & Öktem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.06474)).

Your results matter because they are controlled: every run used the same 130k-weight refiner, the same number of steps and two seeds (`archive/toy/slopes/results_channels.md`). Six training-free channels gave FlatVel-A MAE 0.096 at 25 models against 0.113 for raw gathers at 452; on FlatFault-A, raw gathers plus refraction channels reached **0.078 at 452 models against 0.115 raw**; and trained on faults then tested on flat layers, physics input transferred best (0.130 vs 0.224 raw). Two details already mirror the cross-field findings, since raw plus physics did worse than physics alone at 25–100 models because the raw branch overfits (as in Wang et al.), and raw plus refraction transferred badly (0.250) because the raw branch learns family-specific gather patterns (as Sax et al. found for raw pixels). A rough compute check partly supports the claim. If a 2-s gradient is ten simulations, one raw training pair costs about 1 s to simulate and your channels add about 7 s, so 25 physics models cost about as much as 200 raw ones and still beat 452. The same arithmetic does not favour physics-only on FlatFault-A at 25 models, where the 10-s refraction fit makes the run cost as much as roughly 450 raw models and it loses (0.151 vs 0.115). These are small networks trained on one file per family, far from InversionNet's 0.013–0.017 MAE at 24–48K models.

## Flat-layer channels fail exactly where OpenFWI's geology breaks their assumptions

Each of your channels carries a hypothesis that holds in FlatVel-A. Hyperbola votes and Dix inversion assume flat reflectors, since hyperbolic moveout is exact only for a single layer ([First Break 2021](https://www.earthdoc.org/content/journals/10.3997/1365-2397.fb2021022)) and Dix assumes plane horizontal layers ([CREWES](https://crewes.org/Documents/ResearchReports/1997/1997-29.pdf)). The polarity filter keeps only events with the direct wave's polarity, which is correct only because FlatVel-A has zero velocity decreases; the bottom-layer fallback of "last velocity times 1.13" is FlatVel-A's median jump; the refraction channel fits a two-layer model; and the reflectivity, dip, agreement and gradient channels are all computed in v_geo, so they inherit its errors. None of them flags when its hypothesis fails, so on CurveFault-B and Style-B the network receives confident-looking channels that are wrong and must learn to ignore them from a few hundred examples, which it cannot.

The table below shows the size of the mismatch, measured on one 500-model shard per family of your local OpenFWI copy (`openfwi/data/<family>`); the single FlatFault-A and CurveFault-A shards are mostly two-layer, which may be a generation-group artifact rather than a family-wide property.

What can any channel know in this geometry of 5 shots, 70 receivers over 0.7 km, 15 Hz and 1 s records? The numbers that follow are derived from cited formulas. With a 700 m maximum offset, turning rays reach only **20–145 m** for depth gradients of 0.5 to 4.3 s⁻¹ (circular-ray turning depth, checked against [Harlan's](https://sepwww.stanford.edu/data/media/public/docs/sep89/harlan/paper_html/node1.html) worked example), and head waves arrive first only above shallow, strong-contrast interfaces ([SEG Wiki](https://wiki.seg.org/wiki/Head_waves_(refractions)_and_effect_of_hidden_layer)), consistent with your finding that the direct wave arrives first at every receiver on FlatFault-A. A 10% velocity error changes far-offset reflection moveout by about **30 ms at 200 m depth but only 10 ms at 700 m**, and by 3 ms for the centre shot, against a 15 Hz half period of 33 ms. The half-period criterion means an arrival at 1 s tolerates 3.3% velocity error at 15 Hz but 17% at 3 Hz ([Operto et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.10141)), and the wavenumber relation k = (4πf/c)cos(θ/2) ([Brossier et al.](https://html.rhhz.net/SYWT/HTML/1701-02.htm)) means that 500-m-deep reflections fill vertical wavelengths of only 100–122 m at 15 Hz against 300–366 m at 5 Hz.

The long-wavelength background at depth therefore comes only from reflection kinematics, with their velocity-depth trade-off ([Soupios et al.](https://balkangeophysoc.gr/menu/congresses/oral/10-11-O3-3-soupios-pantelis.PDF)), or from frequencies below about 5 Hz. Noise-free OpenFWI data carry those frequencies, since the 15 Hz Ricker still holds 3 Hz at about −20 dB ([Ricker wavelet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricker_wavelet)), which is a real signal in the benchmark and one that field data rarely offer. Below the deepest reflector only the last reflection's amplitude constrains velocity ([Engquist & Yang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.00031)), low-velocity layers under fast ones produce no head wave ([SEG Wiki](https://wiki.seg.org/wiki/Effect_of_a_hidden_layer)), and common-midpoint fold drops to one within about 85 m of either end of the line.

Mora's identity, inversion = migration + tomography ([Mora 1989](https://pure.kfupm.edu.sa/en/publications/inversion-migration-tomography/)), supplies the organizing principle: every channel is a sensor for a particular wavenumber band in a particular region of the model, and the network's three jobs are to merge the bands, arbitrate where sensors disagree, and fill the null space (the parts of the model the data cannot constrain) with a learned geological prior. This framing explains both failures. In Style-B the information exists, just not as interfaces: Style backgrounds increase linearly with depth ([Feng et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.04007)), so diving waves exist there, unlike in the sharp-layer families, the low band sees the trend, and the migrated image is a band-limited copy of the texture's high wavenumbers. In CurveFault-B interfaces exist but are curved and offset by faults, so flat-layer kinematics fail while wave-equation imaging, coherence and the focusing of fault-tip diffractions, which carries velocity information independent of reflector dip ([Coimbra et al.](https://www.wit.uni-hamburg.de/import/documents/reports/2011/wit2011-coimbra.pdf)), all survive.

Five design rules follow from this. A migration or gradient computed in a stated background is wrong only through that background, which the network can see, so physics operators should replace geology-specific estimates. Every channel should ship with a map of where it is valid, and each geological hypothesis should be tested explicitly in the data, with the test handed to the network. A raw-data branch must stay, because post-critical reflections, head waves and internal multiples lie outside the linear operators but inside the data, which is exactly where your bothR gain came from. Finally, operator calls should be spent where they buy accuracy, once in a second network pass and again at test time, rather than in every training step.

## Fourteen channels, each with its physics, blind spot, price and test

Cost is counted in wave simulations per model, and simulating one raw training pair of five shots costs five. Tier-0 channels need none: they come from eikonal tables or the data directly, and fast marching solves the eikonal equation in O(N log N) ([Sethian](https://math.berkeley.edu/~sethian/2006/History/hold.chart.overview.fastmarching.html)) while fast sweeping does it in O(N) ([Zhang, Zhao & Qian](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/qian/papers/ZhangZhaoQian.pdf)), milliseconds on 4,900 cells. Tier-1 channels cost one forward and one adjoint solve per source, ten simulations per background ([Plessix](https://academic.oup.com/gji/article/167/2/495/559970)); a scan over K backgrounds costs 10K, and iterative methods cost hundreds to thousands. Storing the 70×70×1000 source and receiver wavefields takes about 20 MB per shot and turns band-split images, subsurface-offset images and illumination into post-processing with no extra simulations.

One cost subtlety matters for design. At a fixed reference background, anything that depends only on the background and the acquisition geometry (illumination, point-spread functions, ray density, fold) is identical for every training sample, so it carries no sample-specific information and the network can learn it from a positional encoding. Such channels become informative only when the background (in the second pass) or the acquisition (shot or receiver dropout, field geometry) varies from sample to sample.

### The seismic plane sweep: per-shot images over a scan of backgrounds

MVSNet, a multi-view-stereo network, warps each view's features onto planes at a set of hypothesized depths and measures the variance across views on each plane; because variance is a pooled statistic, one network accepts any number of views ([Yao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.02505)). Wave-equation migration is the seismic warp, since back-propagating one shot's data in a hypothesized background maps it from (time, receiver) into model pixels. Migrating each of the five shots separately at K backgrounds ρ_k · v_ref(z) yields a tensor I[s, k, z, x] indexed by shot, background, depth and position, and at each pixel the background that makes the five shot images agree is the locally correct one. This is classical residual-migration and focusing analysis ([Fomel](https://sepwww.stanford.edu/data/media/public/docs/sep100/sergey2/paper_html/index.html); [SEP depth focusing](https://sep.stanford.edu/data/media/public/docs/sep94/hermes/paper_html/node1.html)) without its flat-dip assumption, because wave-equation migration places curved and faulted reflectors correctly once the background is right. The cross-shot variance at each (k, z, x) is a per-pixel semblance; a 3D CNN regularizes the volume, and a soft-argmax over k turns it into a per-pixel local background ratio ρ*(z, x) with a confidence given by how sharply the volume peaks. ρ* is an interpretable intermediate that can be supervised directly with low-pass(v_true) / v_ref.

The reference background should be anchored on the data: start from the direct-wave v_top at the surface and blend into the all-family mean depth trend. The scan should span ρ log-spaced over 0.7–1.4, much wider than the ±10% Jennings et al. used, because FlatVel-B surface velocities sit far from the mean trend; the second pass then recentres a narrower scan on the first-pass prediction. At K = 5 the scan costs 50 simulations per model, the price of ten raw training pairs, while its kinematic twin (row 4) builds the same volume from oriented points on eikonal tables at zero simulations, so comparing the two at equal K isolates what waveforms add over picks. FNO "velocity continuation" of image gathers could later make dense scans nearly free ([Rex, Yin & Herrmann](https://slim.gatech.edu/node/7333)). The main blind spot is depth resolution, because deep moveout is small. The subsurface-offset image (row 8) complements it, since defocusing there is amplified by a factor 1 + tan²γ for reflection angle γ ([Biondi & Symes](https://sepwww.stanford.edu/sep/biondo/PDF/Journals/Geophysics/adcig-geo-online.pdf)); horizontal-offset gathers blur for steep dips, so the Curve families need vertical lags too.

The construction also reads naturally as geometry processing. Slowness is a conformal Riemannian metric on the model domain, traveltime tables are geodesic distance fields from the sources and receivers, kinematic migration intersects isochron level sets, the scan is a one-parameter family of metrics, and ρ* is the metric correction that makes the five geodesic "views" agree.

### A free test of the flat-layer hypothesis

In a laterally invariant medium the response depends only on source-receiver offset and time, and sources and receivers here sit at the same depth, so the five shot gathers coincide wherever their offsets overlap and the centre shot is mirror-symmetric about its source. For FlatVel models the residual between shots at common offset, and the left-right asymmetry within the centre shot, should therefore vanish up to numerical boundary effects, which takes minutes to verify on FlatVel-A. The residual is non-zero exactly where dips, folds, faults or texture exist, and in noise-free data it costs nothing to compute. Mapped into the model grid by kinematic migration in v_ref, it becomes a per-pixel lateral-heterogeneity map: a physical, region-level version of the per-sample family classifier that a top Kaggle team used to route its predictions ([5th place](https://www.kaggle.com/samson8)). The network can use it as a gate that switches the flat-layer channels (row 5) on or off, and it doubles as an auxiliary target supervised by the true model's lateral-gradient energy. The same logic extends to every geology-specific channel: simulating data from v_geo costs five simulations, and the per-trace residual against the observed gathers shows where v_geo explains the data, so that residual should ship alongside v_geo.

### Signed contrast finds the inversions that kinematics miss

With constant density, impedance contrast equals velocity contrast and the reflection coefficient is R(θ) ≈ ½(Δv/v)sec²θ, so the polarity of each migrated reflector gives the sign of the velocity jump ([Zoeppritz/Shuey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoeppritz_equations)). Inversions are invisible to first-arrival methods and make up 48–50% of the Vel-B interfaces, which makes a signed, illumination-normalized image the single channel most likely to move FlatVel-B and CurveVel-B. A Laplacian filter or the inverse-scattering imaging condition suppresses RTM backscatter at about the cost of a plain image ([Albano et al.](https://sbgf.org.br/revista/index.php/rbgf/article/view/2330)). A recursive "impedance chain", v_{n+1} = v_n(1+R)/(1−R) applied down each image column, turns the signed image into absolute velocity, with errors that grow with depth from transmission loss (two-way transmission is 0.75 at R = 0.5), tuning below λ/4 and wavelet scaling. Your calibrated source, which fits the official gathers to 4–6%, makes the chain usable as a hint with depth-growing uncertainty, never as an answer.

### Confidence channels carry information only when something varies

Your post-critical mask added nothing on FlatFault-A because the network already read unreliability from the reflectivity, which is the expected outcome for a confidence channel that adds no new measurement. Confidence channels earn their place when they come from the data, as the below-deepest-reflector mask (row 12) does, when the acquisition varies through shot or receiver dropout, or when the background is the network's own estimate in the second pass. Their test is calibration rather than accuracy. MC-dropout uncertainty on OpenFWI already correlates with edge gradients at r = 0.546 ([OpenFWI](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.02926)); the goal here is interval coverage that holds inside the null-space mask.

## Align every view in model space, fuse, and close the loop

### Architecture: three view axes and gated evidence groups

The network has three branches. The data branch reads each 1000×70 gather with a per-shot shared encoder and a Fourier embedding of the source position, applies set attention across shots in the Deep Sets form ([Zaheer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06114)), and decodes onto the 70×70 grid through cross-attention from pixel queries; its job is to carry what the linear operators miss. The model-space evidence branch receives the tensor over shots × backgrounds × frequency bands (rows 6–10): a shared 2D encoder processes each slice, mean-and-variance pooling plus attention fuse the shots as in MVSNet, a 3D cost-volume regularizer over backgrounds yields ρ* and its confidence, and attention fuses the bands. The third branch carries the cheap kinematic, structural and confidence maps (rows 2–5 and 11–13), with the legacy flat-layer channels behind their gates. Each group has its own small encoder and auxiliary head, and a per-pixel softmax gate conditioned on the confidence maps decides how much each group contributes before a U-Net decoder. This is the selector-and-combinator design that cured over-reliance on a single cue in background matting ([Sengupta et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.00626)).

The output follows Mora's split, log v = log v_ref + log ρ*_smooth + δ_sharp, with the background term coming from the cost volume and the sharp term from the images and the data branch. Additional heads predict a per-pixel Laplace scale for uncertainty and auxiliary regime maps (lateral heterogeneity, inversion locations, fault mask) defined from the true model rather than from family labels. A trunk of 1–5M parameters keeps runs up to N = 1,600 inside your 90-second loop. Exact mirror augmentation costs one re-simulated centre shot per sample, and set attention with position embeddings handles any shot count. Multi-view stereo reports error falling from 0.441 to 0.380 to 0.371 to 0.370 for 2, 3, 5 and 7 views ([VA-MVSNet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.03001)), so gains saturate near five views and the useful robustness test is training on five shots and testing on three to five.

The second pass recomputes the tier-1 channels at the smoothed first-pass prediction for ten more simulations and feeds the same network with a pass flag. Deep-tomography uses three such passes, TGS two to five, and ASPIRE alternates network and physics summaries in an outer loop ([Orozco et al.](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2405.05398)). Training stage by stage on cached channels keeps the physics out of the optimizer loop while still putting the operator in the inference loop, which is where learned primal-dual found its 2.2 dB.

### Losses: robust pixels, split wavenumbers, cheap kinematic consistency

The pixel loss combines L1 on velocity normalized to [−1, 1], MS-SSIM, and L1 on the spatial gradients of velocity to sharpen interfaces and faults; once the uncertainty head is on, L1 becomes a Laplace negative log-likelihood, of which L1 is the fixed-scale special case. A top Kaggle team trained a similar per-pixel confidence head on its own absolute error ([4th place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/waveform-inversion/writeups/greysnow-4th-place-solution-for-the-gwi-competitio)). Auxiliary losses supervise the intermediates directly: the background head against low-pass(v_true), the sharp head against high-pass(v_true), and ρ* against low-pass(v_true) / v_ref. Each group's auxiliary head should be weighted by Gradient-Blending's ratio of overfitting to generalization rather than equally, since equal weights dropped accuracy to 70.8% in Wang et al. ([Wang et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1905.12681)).

Cheap physics consistency can run every batch. A differentiable eikonal solve compares the predicted model's first-arrival times with the picked first breaks at milliseconds per model. Cross-shot kinematic consistency, migrating oriented points on the predicted model's traveltime tables and asking the shots to agree, is your overlap objective; its minimum lies at the truth, but it is biased slow on strong contrasts and your related depth-spread variant could be gamed, so its weight should stay small. A cross-branch term penalizing disagreement between the data-branch and evidence-branch predictions adds a third check: in vision, this kind of consistency energy correlates with error at r = 0.67 and doubles as a detector for out-of-distribution inputs ([Zamir et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Zamir_Robust_Learning_Through_Cross-Task_Consistency_CVPR_2020_paper.html)).

Expensive physics consistency, a low-frequency waveform misfit through differentiable finite differences using trace-wise W2 or envelope misfits, belongs on a fraction of batches. Its most valuable use is self-supervised adaptation to an unlabeled held-out family, because UPFWI's benefit came mostly from unlabeled data and TGS needed exactly this kind of loss for field transfer. The adversarial loss stays out of the core design: the perception-distortion theorem guarantees a fidelity cost ([Blau & Michaeli](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/html/Blau_The_Perception-Distortion_Tradeoff_CVPR_2018_paper.html)) and plausible false structure is the documented risk ([Bhadra et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00646v1)), so it enters only as an ablation paired with a hallucination check.

### Robustness: drop, corrupt and gate every group

Each evidence group should be dropped with probability about 0.1 during training; in the ModDrop gesture-recognition study this lifted accuracy with a missing input from 38% to 93% at no cost when all inputs were present ([Neverova et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1501.00102)). Training should also simulate acquisition faults: three to five shots, 5–25% missing traces (UPFWI's velocity MSE rose about tenfold between 5% and 25% missing traces), added noise, and source-wavelet shifts, since InversionNet degrades under small changes of source frequency ([Guo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08005v1)). The physics channels themselves need corruption too, computed at randomly perturbed backgrounds and with spurious or missing interfaces injected into v_geo, so the network learns to treat them as evidence rather than answers; background matting needed the same kind of perturbation of its background-plate input to stop trusting it blindly. At inference, one trust scalar per group gives a manual override, and ablating each group in turn exposes "holes" where an over-trusted cue fails.

### Test-time refinement gated by the half-period rule

Refinement starts by simulating data from the network's prediction (five simulations) and measuring the per-trace time lag against the observed gathers in the 15 Hz band. If every lag is within half a period (33 ms), multiscale L2 FWI can run directly. Otherwise the run should start at 2–3 Hz or with a misfit convex in time shifts, such as optimal transport ([Engquist & Yang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.00031)) or the envelope ([Wu & Chen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.05275)), switching to L2 only once the lags fall inside the basin, the ordering the foundation-model FWI study found essential. The regularizer penalizes departure from the network weighted by its predicted uncertainty, ‖(v − v_net)/b‖₁, so confident pixels stay put while uncertain ones follow the data, plus structure-oriented smoothing along the predicted dip, the classical geology regularizer ([Trinh et al.](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2018/EGU2018-5440.pdf?pdf)); reparameterizing through the network itself (DR-FWI) or its latent space is the alternative. Refinement costs about 750–3,000 simulations per model, seconds to minutes on this grid with a compiled GPU solver, so it should be budgeted for evaluation subsets. Every update should be reported split into the part the data constrain and the part in the null space, because changes inside the below-reflector mask cannot come from the data and flag hallucination ([Bhadra et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00646v1)). Running refinement from three starts (the network prediction, v_ref and the oracle-smoothed truth) ties your numbers to PSG and DiffusionVel through the oracle start while filling the gap the literature leaves open through the network start.

## A staged plan that can falsify every channel

Matched budget here means the same trunk size within 10%, the same optimizer steps and augmentation, and the same hyperparameter-tuning budget for every input type. Every learning curve has two x-axes, training pairs N and total simulations including data generation and channels, and every point uses three seeds with paired per-model bootstrap confidence intervals. Each curve is summarized by its data-equivalence factor, the number of raw pairs needed to match physics at N, and by its fitted power-law exponent. Compute-matched curves are the honest bar, because a physics channel must beat spending the same simulations on more raw pairs. That comparison needs a velocity-model generator per family, and OpenFWI publishes the generating equations but no generator code was found, so where no generator exists the channel cost should be reported as overhead on the official subsets.

The metrics follow the OpenFWI convention, MAE, RMSE and SSIM on velocity normalized to [−1, 1] (MAE × 1500 ≈ m/s), extended with region-wise MAE in the shallow zone (<150 m), at mid-depth, below the deepest reflector, within two cells of interfaces, in fault zones, just below inversions and at the lateral edges (<100 m). They also include interface-position error and boundary F-score, 90% interval coverage with the rank correlation between predicted uncertainty and error, and the fraction of traces within the half-period lag, a physics-consistency measure that doubles as the refinement gate.

Plain leave-one-family-out leaks, because each A/B pair shares a generator, so leave-one-geology-out, which holds out both members of a pair, is the stricter test. The all-A-to-all-B split tests inversions and sharp contrasts the network has never seen, and the Vel-and-Fault-to-Style split tests non-stratigraphic geology. The cache should start with four families, FlatVel-B, CurveFault-B and Style-B plus FlatVel-A as a sanity check; tier-0 and tier-1 channels for 6,400 models per family then cost about 256K simulations, roughly 14 h at your current 0.2 s per simulation and minutes to a few hours with a batched GPU engine.

Each design choice makes a prediction the plan can falsify. The scan and kinematic channels should lower mid-depth long-wavelength error on the Curve and Fault families, and signed contrast should lower error below inversions on Vel-B. The physics advantage should shrink with N in distribution but persist on held-out geology, as Sax et al. predict, while the raw branch should remain necessary below strong contrasts. At equal simulations the second pass should beat adding more first-pass channels, as learned primal-dual predicts, and refinement gains should concentrate in illuminated regions while leaving masked pixels unchanged. A result against any of these predictions is itself informative.

## Conclusion

Your flat-layer channels did not fail for lack of physics; they failed because they delivered answers derived under FlatVel-A's hypotheses with no statement of where those hypotheses hold. The fix is to change what a channel is: operator outputs computed in a stated background, each paired with its validity and a data test of its own assumptions, with the network doing the arbitration a classical processor does by hand. Seen this way, the closest analogy is multi-view stereo rather than the green screen, because the adjoint is the warp into a common frame, shots and trial backgrounds are the views and depth hypotheses, and velocity analysis becomes plane-sweep cost-volume regression, a geometric problem with a mature toolset that, to my knowledge, has never been posed this way for OpenFWI.

The literature also says where each investment pays. Physics channels pay in small-data and held-out-geology regimes, the operator in the inference loop pays in accuracy, raw data pay where the linear physics stops (below strong contrasts and in post-critical energy), and data diversity pays everywhere, which makes it the baseline any physics channel must beat at equal simulations. The first sample- and compute-matched learning curves across all ten families, run under leave-one-geology-out, would settle a question the field has argued only qualitatively, and that is a stronger contribution than any single new channel.
